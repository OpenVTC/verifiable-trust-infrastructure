Verifiable Trust Community · Administration

Who runs a community, and who stops them

How administrator access works in a VTC: a single owner or a team, how each one proves who they are, and the controls that keep a single stolen credential, or a single rogue administrator, from taking the community over.

Authority = an ACL row, read at every act Step-up bound to one operation VTI-APV-014 second-admin consent Break-glass offline & audited

01 · The model

An administrator is an ACL row

A DID is an administrator because the community's access-control list says so. Every act is authorized by reading that row at the moment it runs. Sessions, console keys and credentials carry no authority of their own, and only administrators can sign in to the console.

What is a context?

A label on an ACL entry, nothing more

Contexts are path-like labels (events covers events/2026). The community keeps no list of them, and no community resource belongs to one: members, joins, vetting, credentials and rooms are all community-wide. They decide only whether an admin is scoped or unrestricted, which ACL entries a scoped admin can see and change, and whose step-up passkeys it can list. In the VTA a context is a key hierarchy; the VTC borrows the ACL model, not that meaning.

See them in Access control: the Contexts column and Filter by context, or vtc acl list offline. "all" in that column is true only for admins; a member with no contexts has none.

02 · Proving who you are

Three ways in, one signing key per browser

After sign-in the console signs every act as a Trust Task document with a console signing key. It is generated in the browser, can't be exported, and is enrolled as a delegation from your admin DID. It lasts at most 30 days, you can hold at most five, and it only works in the console. It proves you have the browser, never that you are present.

03 · One administrator or several

Run with at least two unrestricted admins

Day-to-day administration works with one administrator. Making someone else unrestricted needs another unrestricted administrator to agree, and a single administrator has nobody to ask.

One unrestricted admin

Works, but can't grow online

Granting unrestricted admin is refused: "needs consent from 1 other unrestricted admin(s), and this community has 0 (VTI-APV-014)". The ways forward:

  • Grant scoped admin (set contexts), which needs only your step-up
  • Offline, once: vtc acl add --did … --role admin with the daemon stopped (audited)
  • At install: name co_admin_did in vtc setup

It is also a single point of failure: lose the passkey and the only way back is offline.

Two or more

Everything online

One administrator proposes, another approves, and break-glass isn't needed again unless you drop back to one.

  • With three or more, raise acl.unrestricted_admin_consent_threshold (1–16, changeable at runtime) to require several approvals
  • A threshold the community can't meet is refused when you set it
  • So is a removal that would leave the threshold unmeetable
Run by one person

Single-administrator mode

Set on the host at install (vtc setup --single-admin; [acl] single_admin_mode, never online). Where nobody but you could consent, your step-up bound to the operation authorizes it. A second eligible admin makes consent apply again. A banner on every page, and every waived consent audited Critical (VTI-APV-022).

04 · Protection against a bad administrator

Assume one credential is stolen

Each control limits what one administrator, or one stolen key, can do alone.

👆

Step-up bound to one operation

Granting or promoting admin, minting an admin invite, inviting a step-up passkey, and git break-glass all need a live, user-verified gesture from the actor's own passkey. The gesture is made for a digest of that exact operation, can be used once within 300 s, and raises no session. A console key can redeem a gesture but never make one.

stops: a script holding your console key
🤝

Second-party consent (VTI-APV-014)

Making or widening anyone to unrestricted needs another unrestricted admin's approval. The requester is never an approver. The approval is bound to that payload, re-checked when used, and spent once.

stops: one admin minting a co-conspirator
⚖️

Separation of duties

No self-grant, self-promotion, self-rewrite or self-revoke. No entry can outlive the one who wrote it. Every promotion runs the role-change policy and its host checks.

stops: self-elevation
🛡️

Attrition guards

Removals and demotions run one at a time under a lock. The last admin, or the last unrestricted admin, can't be removed, and nor can anyone whose removal would leave the threshold unmeetable. A removed admin loses their sessions and gets a signed notice. Removing, demoting or narrowing an unrestricted admin needs the other unrestricted admins' approval (VTI-APV-019). With only two, nobody is left to approve, so it lands after a 24 h cooling-off (acl.removal_cooling_off) that the requester can cancel and the subject sees coming but can't block. First to act wins.

stops: stripping the other admins
📜

Everything audited, break-glass included

Every grant, revocation, promotion, invite, passkey and consent step has an audit row. Offline writers, emergency bootstrap included, leave a marker, and the daemon records it as AclBreakGlassWritten at its next start, naming the command, the DID and the host. It also raises it in Actions as an item the administrators must acknowledge, with a Critical banner that can't be dismissed until they do (VTI-VTC-023). The operator can't be constrained, only made visible.

stops: silent changes
🎟️

Bounded invites and notices

Invites are single use, last at most 24 h, and come with a claim code sent separately. Five wrong codes void a step-up passkey invite, or an install token at claim 0.3. Members are told when a step-up passkey is enrolled or revoked for them, and by whom. An admin whose authority is reduced gets a signed authority-reduced notice: what changed, who decided, and whether anyone else agreed.

stops: hijacked invites

The offline break-glass bypasses every control above and needs the host itself. Protect the host the way you protect the community.

05 · How a second admin agrees

Propose, approve, complete

The requester's own gesture comes first, so a thief holding only their signing key can't make the other administrators' devices ring. The operation is parked as an action; the approval that reaches the threshold runs it, after the VTC checks everything again.

First admin, then a second on the browser plugin

Alice runs the install and signs in with a passkey. Bob uses the VTA browser plugin and administers as his wallet persona did:webvh:…:bob.

1
Bob · before setup

Bob sends Alice his DID

He installs the browser plugin, onboards it to his VTA, picks the persona he will administer as, and sends Alice its DID. If the persona's DID is published, Alice resolves it to check she has the right one.

2
Alice · install

Install the community with Bob as co-admin

vtc setup                      # or: vtc setup --from setup.toml
# in the TOML:
co_admin_did = "did:webvh:…:bob"
  1. Setup mints Alice's admin did:key and prints a one-time install URL and a separate claim code, both valid for 15 minutes.
  2. She opens Claim Admin Passkey, enters the code and registers a passkey. The VTC writes two unrestricted admin rows, hers and Bob's ("co-admin (install bootstrap)").
  3. Sign in with passkey, then Set up signing with her passkey.

The claim code is what protects this step, not the URL. Send them separately.

A founder who uses only a wallet picks the other option on the same install page (claim 0.3): the wallet signs as their own DID and the plugin's approver device becomes their step-up factor.

Alice · only if Bob wasn't named at install

vtc acl add --did did:webvh:…:bob \
  --role admin --label "Bob"

4
Bob · browser plugin

Sign in with the wallet and set up signing

  1. Sign in as a VTA identity → his persona. His VTA signs the sign-in.
  2. Set up signing with your wallet: the VTA authorizes a new console key in this browser.

Bob can now do every administrative act that doesn't need a step-up.

5
Alice → Bob

Invite Bob to register a passkey

Without a passkey at this VTC, every step-up act is refused for Bob, and he can't add the first one himself. Alice:

  1. Access control → Admin invites → Invite admin → Bob's DID → Mint invite. Bob is already an admin, so this grants nothing and needs no consent.
  2. Sends the URL and the claim code by different channels.
  3. Bob opens it, enters the code and registers a passkey (AdminPasskeyRegistered).

Bob's step-up now works whichever way he signs in.

6
Alice · cnm

Make sure an approver can sign

Approvals are signed by the admin DID's own key, never a console key. In the console the browser wallet signs them; an admin without a wallet uses cnm consent approve --action, which needs the cnm Client DID to have its own unrestricted admin row (see the bootstrap runbook). A passkey can be added as an extra factor, never instead of the signature.

7
Bob proposes · Alice approves

A third admin, entirely online

  1. Bob makes Carol unrestricted in the console and confirms with his passkey. The console shows "Sent for approval — 1 of 1 must approve within 72 hours".
  2. Alice sees it under Actions → Waiting for me and approves with her wallet, or from cnm:
cnm actions list --view waiting
cnm consent approve --action <actionId>
  1. Alice's approval runs Bob's stored operation. Carol has her row, and Bob's completed action shows him, once, an approver enrolment invite for Carol. He sends her the URL and, separately, the claim code. Carol repeats step 4 and enrols her approver device from the invite.

07 · Known gaps

What isn't built yet

Step-up

Approver step-up needs the plugin

The VTC accepts a step-up approver's statement; driving it from the console waits on the plugin's approveStepUp. Workaround: step 5's passkey.

Approving

Console approves with a passkey

The VTC accepts approverSigned decisions, but the plugin signs approver statements for enrolment only. cnm and other clients can send them.

Cooling-off

Not pushed to the subject

The subject of a two-admin removal learns of it from the console banner and the action list; a "pending" notice needs a specification first. requireRequesterAtCompletion is not built either.

Three holes, now closed

Found by a survey of every VTC operation on 2 October 2026. Together they let one compromised unrestricted admin get round the second-admin rule: remove the others, lower the threshold, then promote at will.

Hole 1

Stripping the other admins

acl/revoke, demoting with acl/change-role, and members/admin-remove need no second admin. One admin can remove every other admin except the last.

Hole 2

Lowering the threshold

config/patch or config/import can set unrestricted_admin_consent_threshold back to 1 with no consent. Only a value the community can't meet is refused.

Hole 3

Policy is open to scoped admins

policy/upsert and activate accept any admin, and replace the rules for role changes, removal, joins and more. The default ban on removing admins is one of those rules.

08 · What's coming

Wallet administrators as first-class

The design note docs/05-design-notes/vtc-approver-step-up.md (VTC side built; the plugin's step-up signing and phone approvers to come) lets the browser plugin's approver identity be an administrator's step-up factor. That identity is a separate did:key whose key is unlocked only by a biometric or hardware gesture. Once the plugin signs step-ups, Bob needs no passkey at the VTC.

The factor

Your own approver device

A new step-up evidence kind, approverSigned. It is a re-authentication, not a consent: the device is your second factor, not another party (VTI-APV-003). The VTC checks the device's signature itself and never relies on the VTA's word.

The first-factor problem

Four anchors to enrol

  • Install: claim 0.3 under your existing DID, for a wallet-only founder
  • Invite: another admin, behind their own step-up
  • Self-service: from a factor you already hold
  • Offline: vtc admin enrol-approver, audited
Beyond the browser

Phone approvers (phase 2)

The VTC pushes the request over TSP or DIDComm to the phone, which signs the same statement. This needs the VTC registered as a trusted issuer on the device at enrolment.

The action list: N-of-M approvals that complete themselves

Built (docs/05-design-notes/vtc-action-list.md, phases A1 and A2). An operation that needs other admins' agreement is parked as an action instead of refused. Approvers find it in the console whenever they next sign in. When N of them have approved, the VTC re-checks everything and runs it, and it disappears from everyone else's list.

In the console

See what's waiting

Actions2

A badge with your count, a banner after sign-in, and (2) in the tab title. The page has tabs Waiting for me, Requested by me and History. Offline writes appear as items to Acknowledge, each named by the record type vtc/operator/offline-write/0.1. Pushes to devices are off by default (acl.consent_request_push); the list is the source of truth.

Approving

Your own factor, never a console key

  • Wallet (console) or cnm: the decision is signed by the admin DID's own key
  • Passkey: the console adds an assertion over the decision's challenge
  • Approver device (built): the plugin's approveDecision signs an approverSigned statement over your salted digest, and the console sends it ahead of a passkey; cnm and other clients can too
Rules

One rule list, keyed on task

Same model as the VTA: each rule names a Trust Task URI, its approvers, N, whether the requester is excluded, and a lifetime. Built-in rules can be tightened, never loosened. Changing the rules is itself an N-of-M action.

Everything that could use it

Built in · protects the admin set

N-of-M from day one

  • Making or widening an unrestricted admin (APV-014)
  • Removing, demoting or narrowing an unrestricted admin (closes hole 1)
  • Lowering the consent threshold (closes hole 2)
  • Policy changes for role change, removal, join, recognition and git-ns (closes hole 3)
  • Restoring a backup, which replaces the ACL

With only two admins, a removal has nobody else to approve it. It goes ahead after a 24 h cooling-off (built): the subject is sent a reduction-pending notice when it is parked and sees it as a coolingOff action counting down to landsAt; when it lands they get the authority-reduced notice, and it is audited Critical.

Optional · by rule

High-impact or irreversible

  • Backup export; purging members
  • Extending the VTC's DID log
  • Join criteria changes (review → automatic)
  • Granting vetters; auto-grant settings
  • Endorsement and personhood revocation
  • Discarding trust-registry sync jobs
  • Other policy; restart
  • git-ns unbind, reseat, repo transfer, granting ns.admin or own
Existing queues · move in

Already waiting on a person

  • git-ns break-glass: Ratify or Revoke; never expires into acceptance
  • Join review (admission: review): N = 1 by default
  • Vetting withdrawal review: Keep member, or Start removal
Deliberately not

Must stay fast, or isn't an admin decision

  • Incident response: revoking console keys or sessions, revoking invites. These remove access, never grant it
  • Rooms: authority is the owner chain, not the ACL
  • Forge or member link waits: a machine, not a person
  • Profile, website, schemas, invitations: optional at most
