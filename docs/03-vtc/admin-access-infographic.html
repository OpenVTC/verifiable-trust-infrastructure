Verifiable Trust Community · Administration

Who runs a community, and who stops them

How administrator access works in a VTC: roles and capabilities, a single owner or a team, how each administrator proves who they are, and the controls that keep a single stolen credential, or a single rogue administrator, from taking the community over.

Authority = capabilities on an ACL entry, read at every act Step-up bound to one operation VTI-APV-018 N-of-M approvals that run themselves Break-glass offline, audited & acknowledged

01 · The model

An administrator is an ACL entry with a role

A DID is an administrator because the community's access-control list gives it an administrative role. Every act asks one question of that entry at the moment it runs: does it hold the capability this act needs, at this resource? Sessions, console keys and credentials carry no authority of their own.

  • An entry holds the full ceiling, or a narrower set of its capabilities
  • Act and approve authority are separate: the least-privilege approver acts nowhere
Capabilities

Twenty powers, some qualified by a resource

One registry, fixed in code. A qualifier narrows a capability to a git namespace or repository, a policy purpose or a join criterion. Those marked authority-conferring let their holder create authority, so granting one always takes another holder's approval.

  • Confer authority: vtc.roles.assign, vtc.approvals.admin, vtc.config.admin, vtc.backup.restore, vtc.did.admin, git.ns.admin, vtc.policy.admin for authority purposes
  • Day to day: members, joins, invitations, credentials, vetting, the website, registry, audit, sessions, repositories
Custom roles

When the built-ins don't fit

vtc/roles/define and delete, through the action list: take vtc.roles.assign + vtc.approvals.admin, and a ceiling no wider than the definers hold. A role in use can't be deleted; an entry naming an undefined role confers nothing. See them on the Roles page.

Git rights

Capabilities beside the role

Namespace admin, repo create / own / maintain and commit signing are resource grants on the holder's entry, granted only by git-ns/right/grant and bounded by the granter's own entry. A member with no admin role can own a repository; the bridge holds an application entry that never signs in.

Grants are delegations

A departed granter's grants go to review

Granter removed, narrowed or expired: its grants become one acl.grants.review item — Re-affirm or Withdraw, withdrawn at the deadline. Git grants go to review only when their granter leaves the community.

No contexts at a VTC (VTI-VTC-010). Authority is narrowed with resource qualifiers, never a context list; vtc acl add refuses --contexts.

02 · Proving who you are

Three ways in, one signing key per browser

After sign-in the console signs every act as a Trust Task document with a console signing key. It is generated in the browser, can't be exported, and is enrolled as a delegation from your admin DID, approved in your wallet or with your passkey. It lasts at most 30 days, you can hold at most five, and it only works in the console. It proves you have the browser, never that you are present.

03 · One administrator or several

Run with at least two community admins

Day-to-day administration works with one administrator. Granting an authority-conferring capability needs another holder of it to agree, and a single administrator has nobody to ask — unless the community is installed for exactly that.

One community admin

Can't confer authority alone

Making someone a community-admin online is refused: "needs consent from 1 other holder(s) of vtc.roles.assign, … (VTI-APV-018)". The ways forward:

  • Single-administrator mode, set at install (right)
  • A narrower role — moderator, auditor, vetting lead, credential officer — on your step-up alone
  • At install: name co_admin_did in vtc setup
  • Offline, once: vtc acl add --did … --role admin with the daemon stopped (audited, acknowledged)
Run by one person

Single-administrator mode

Set on the host (vtc setup --single-admin; [acl] single_admin_mode, never online). Where nobody but you could consent, your step-up bound to the operation authorizes it (VTI-APV-022).

  • A second eligible admin makes consent apply again, automatically
  • Removals and narrowings keep their cooling-off
  • A SINGLE ADMIN MODE banner on every page; every waived consent audited Critical
Two or more

Everything online

One administrator proposes, another approves, and break-glass isn't needed again unless you drop back to one. With three or more, raise acl.unrestricted_admin_consent_threshold (1–16, changeable at runtime) to require several approvals. A threshold the community can't meet is refused when you set it, and so is a removal that would leave it unmeetable.

04 · Protection against a bad administrator

Assume one credential is stolen

Each control limits what one administrator, or one stolen key, can do alone.

👆

Step-up bound to one operation

Granting or widening authority, admin invites, removing or narrowing an admin, lowering the threshold, authority policy, custom roles, a restore's commit, enrolling factors for others, and git break-glass all need a live step-up from the actor's own factor: a user-verified passkey, or their approver device. It is made for a digest of that exact operation, can be used once within 300 s, and raises no session. A console key can redeem a step-up but never make one.

stops: a script holding your console key
🤝

Second-party consent (VTI-APV-018)

Granting any authority-conferring capability needs the approval of another holder of it, at a covering resource. The requester is never an approver. The operation waits in the action list and runs itself on the N-th approval, after everything is checked again.

stops: one admin minting a co-conspirator
⚖️

Separation of duties

No self-grant, self-promotion, self-rewrite or self-revoke; rolling your own entry to a new key is the one exception, and changes nothing it holds. No entry can outlive the one who wrote it, and a grant can't exceed its granter. Every promotion runs the role-change policy and its host checks.

stops: self-elevation
🛡️

Attrition guards and the cooling-off

Removals and demotions run one at a time under a lock. The last holder of vtc.roles.assign can't be removed, nor anyone whose removal would leave the threshold unmeetable. Removing or narrowing an admin needs the other holders' approval (VTI-APV-019). With nobody else to approve, it lands after a 24 h cooling-off (acl.removal_cooling_off): the requester can cancel, the subject is sent a reduction-pending notice and sees it coming but can't block it. First to act wins.

stops: stripping the other admins
📜

Everything audited, break-glass included

Every grant, revocation, promotion, key rotation, invite, factor and action step has an audit row. Offline writers, emergency bootstrap included, leave a marker; at the next start the daemon audits it and raises it in Actions as an item the administrators must acknowledge, with a Critical banner that can't be dismissed until they do (VTI-VTC-023). The operator can't be constrained, only made visible.

stops: silent changes
🎟️

Bounded invites and notices

Invites are single use, last at most 24 h, and come with a claim code sent separately. Five wrong codes void a step-up invite, or an install token at claim 0.3. Members are told when a step-up factor is enrolled or revoked for them, and by whom. An admin whose authority is reduced gets a signed authority-reduced notice: what changed, who decided, and whether anyone else agreed.

stops: hijacked invites

Three holes, closed

Found by a survey of every VTC operation on 2 October 2026: together they let one compromised administrator get round the second-admin rule — remove the others, lower the threshold, then promote at will. Each is now a second-party action of its own.

Hole 1 · closed

Stripping the other admins

acl/revoke, demotion, narrowing and members/admin-remove take a step-up, and another holder's approval or a cooling-off (VTI-APV-019).

Hole 2 · closed

Lowering the threshold

Lowering it by config/patch or import needs the current threshold's approvals (VTI-APV-020). Raising it stays immediate.

Hole 3 · closed

Rewriting authority policy

Policy takes vtc.policy.admin, and the purposes that decide authority — role change, removal, join, cross-community roles, git-ns — need another holder's approval (VTI-VTC-022).

How a wallet administrator steps up

The browser plugin's approver identity — a separate did:key per community, unlocked only by a biometric or hardware gesture — is the administrator's step-up factor (VTI-APV-015). It is a re-authentication, not a consent: the device is your second factor, not another party. The VTC checks the device's signature itself.

The first-factor problem

Four anchors to enrol (VTI-APV-016)

  • Install: claim 0.3 under your existing DID, for a wallet-only founder
  • Invite: another admin, behind their own step-up (Members → Invite to enrol an approver)
  • Self-service: from a factor you already hold (My passkeys → Approver devices)
  • Offline: vtc admin enrol-approver, audited and acknowledged
Rules of the factor

Bound once, to one subject

  • Nobody invites themselves; a revoked approver DID is never bound again; at most five per subject
  • Once you hold a dedicated factor, your session passkeys stop counting
  • Every admin made by a completed action is issued an enrolment invite automatically

The offline break-glass bypasses every control above and needs the host itself. Protect the host the way you protect the community.

05 · The action list

Propose, approve, complete

The requester's own step-up comes first, so a thief holding only their signing key can't make the other administrators' devices ring. The operation is parked as an action; the approval that reaches the threshold runs it, after the VTC checks everything again (VTI-APV-017).

In the console

See what's waiting

Actions2

A badge with your count, a banner after sign-in, and (2) in the tab title. Tabs Waiting for me, Requested by me and History; cnm actions list shows the same. Pushes to devices are off by default (acl.consent_request_push); the list is the source of truth.

Approving

Your own DID, never a console key

  • Wallet (console) or cnm: the decision is signed by the admin DID itself
  • Approver device: the plugin's approveDecision adds an approverSigned statement over your salted digest
  • Else a passkey assertion; an extra factor never replaces the signature
Four kinds of item

One list

  • Approval — N of M, then it runs
  • Queue — one decision on a break-glass, a referred join or a withdrawn vetting statement; never expires
  • Cooling-off — a reduction nobody else could approve; lands at landsAt unless its requester cancels
  • Acknowledge — an operator's offline write (vtc/operator/offline-write/0.1), with a Critical banner

What waits in the list

Approvals · built in

Protecting the admin set

  • Granting or widening an authority-conferring capability; inviting an admin (VTI-APV-018)
  • Removing, demoting or narrowing an admin (VTI-APV-019)
  • Lowering the consent threshold (VTI-APV-020)
  • Policy for role change, removal, join, cross-community roles and git-ns (VTI-VTC-022)
  • Defining or deleting a custom role; committing a backup restore
  • A departed granter's grants: Re-affirm or Withdraw
Deliberately not

Must stay fast, or isn't an admin decision

  • Incident response: revoking console keys or sessions, revoking invites. These remove access, never grant it
  • Rooms: authority is the owner chain, not the ACL
  • Forge or member link waits: a machine, not a person
  • Day-to-day roles (moderator, auditor, …): your step-up alone, since they confer nothing

06 · Walkthrough

First admin, then a second on the browser plugin

Alice runs the install and signs in with a passkey. Bob uses the VTA browser plugin, administers as his wallet persona did:webvh:…:bob, and steps up with the plugin's approver device.

AliceBobBoth / VTC
1
Bob · before setup

Bob sends Alice his DID

He installs the browser plugin, onboards it to his VTA, picks the persona he will administer as, and sends Alice its DID. If the persona's DID is published, Alice resolves it to check she has the right one. (From cnm instead: cnm community add mints a DID for this community alone.)

2
Alice · install

Install the community with Bob as co-admin

vtc setup                      # or: vtc setup --from setup.toml
# in the TOML:
co_admin_did = "did:webvh:…:bob"
  1. Setup mints Alice's admin did:key and prints a one-time install URL and a separate claim code, both valid for 15 minutes.
  2. She opens Claim Admin Passkey, enters the code and registers a passkey. The VTC writes two community-admin entries, hers and Bob's ("co-admin (install bootstrap)").
  3. Sign in with passkey, then Set up signing with her passkey.

The claim code is what protects this step, not the URL. Send them separately.

A founder who uses only a wallet picks the other option on the same install page (claim 0.3): the wallet signs as their own DID and the plugin's approver device becomes their step-up factor.

3
Alice · only if Bob wasn't named at install

Give Bob an entry

A narrower role, online
Access control → Add entry → DID, role such as moderator → Create entry → Alice's step-up. In single-administrator mode, community-admin works this way too.
Community admin, offline once
vtc acl add --did did:webvh:…:bob \
  --role admin --label "Bob"
# daemon stopped; audited and
# acknowledged at next start
4
Bob · browser plugin

Sign in with the wallet and set up signing

  1. Sign in as a VTA identity → his persona. His VTA signs the sign-in.
  2. Set up signing with your wallet: the VTA authorizes a new console key in this browser.

Bob can now do every administrative act that doesn't need a step-up. One that does tells him he holds no step-up factor, and names the invite and the offline command.

5
Alice → Bob

Invite Bob to enrol an approver device

  1. Members → Bob → Invite to enrol an approver → Alice's own step-up.
  2. She sends the URL and the claim code by different channels.
  3. Bob opens /admin/enrol-approver, types the code, and approves twice in the plugin: his wallet signs as his DID, the approver signs the enrolment.

Bob now steps up with the plugin. Prefer a passkey? Access control → Admin invites → Invite admin lets him register one; no consent needed, since he is already an admin.

6
Alice · cnm

Make sure an approver can sign

Approvals are signed by the admin DID's own key, never a console key. In the console the browser wallet signs them; an admin without a wallet uses cnm consent approve --action, which needs the cnm identity to have its own administrator entry (see the bootstrap runbook). An approver device or passkey can be added as an extra factor, never instead of the signature.

7
Bob proposes · Alice approves

A third admin, entirely online

  1. Bob makes Carol a community-admin in the console and confirms with his approver device. The console shows "Sent for approval — 1 of 1 must approve within 72 hours".
  2. Alice sees it under Actions → Waiting for me and approves with her wallet, or from cnm:
cnm actions list --view waiting
cnm consent approve --action <actionId>
  1. Alice's approval runs Bob's stored operation. Carol has her entry, and Bob's completed action shows him, once, an approver enrolment invite for Carol. He sends her the URL and, separately, the claim code. Carol repeats step 4 and enrols her approver device from the invite.

08 · Still open

What isn't built yet

Wire

Record types for review items

The departed-granter review, the boot migration's acknowledge item and the three queues name their payload with placeholder urn:openvtc: URIs; no record type is specified for them yet.

Keys

Combined VTC + VTA rotation

A cnm identity bound to a community VTA (--vta-did) isn't rotated: its VTC and VTA entries would have to move together.

Approvals

requireRequesterAtCompletion

Actions complete on the N-th approval; making the requester finish with a fresh step-up is designed, not built.

Rollover

Hand-off markers

VTI-ACL-054 – 058. acl/swap-key is self-rotation (VTI-CLT-025 – 032); hand-off markers aren't implemented.

Queues

Join review with several deciders

Every queue item is decided by one holder of the capability; asking for more than one decision for some join criteria isn't built.

Rules

The approvals rule list

Approval rules are fixed in code; the rule list and the optional rules a community could add are designed, not built. Phone approvers are phase 2.
