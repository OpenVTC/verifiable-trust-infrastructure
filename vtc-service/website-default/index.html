What is a Verifiable Trust Community?

A community that issues its members credentials they hold themselves, in their own wallet, under their own identifier. Anyone can check a membership without asking the community, and the community keeps a tamper-evident record of every decision it makes about who may do what.

  1. 1 Get a wallet. A Verifiable Trust Agent or the browser wallet holds your keys.
  2. 2 Join. Scan the community DID and request membership.
  3. 3 Sign in. Use the member portal with a passkey or your wallet.

Capabilities

What this community runs on

Every grant, credential and decision is a signed record, checked against the community's live access list on every request.

New

Git repositories, governed across forges

The community decides who may create repositories, who owns each one and whose signed commits its checks accept. It then projects those rights onto every forge it has bound, and publishes them to its trust registry. Rights follow a repository's id, so a rename keeps them and a new repository under an old name inherits nothing.

New

Verifiable data rooms

Shared spaces whose membership is decided by credentials the room itself issued, not by a roster on this server. Sealed rooms store only ciphertext the host cannot read, and every membership change moves the room to a new key epoch.

Access management

Capability-based roles with ceilings. Sensitive acts take a step-up bound to that one operation, answered with a passkey or an approver device. Changes to other administrators wait for second-party consent. Everything lands in an HMAC-chained audit trail.

Membership credentials

Members hold W3C verifiable credentials in their own wallet, under their own DID. A public status list lets anyone check that a membership is still current, without asking the community who is looking.

Cross-community recognition

Communities recognise each other's credentials and publish what they trust to a trust registry, so a role earned in one community can count in another.
